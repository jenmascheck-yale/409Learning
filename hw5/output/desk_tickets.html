Ticket 101 — Bulldog tee for Tauhid Zaman

Requester: Tauhid Zaman · wants 1 × CC-TEE-WHITE, size S.

Why this is tricky: size S is sold out (0 on hand), and the reprint vendor (Bulldog Print Co, vendor 1) has an open $840 invoice (#501) — a vendor won't ship new product while an invoice is unpaid, so the invoice has to be cleared first.

Expected

Boss calls first: Inventory. Ticket 101 is a stock question — can we fill a size-S tee? The Boss should start with Inventory to learn the real on-hand count before anyone talks about money or the customer.

Delegations I expect:

  1. Boss → Inventory: check size-S stock for CC-TEE-WHITE. Inventory finds 0 on hand (sold out), identifies the reprint vendor (vendor 1, 5-day lead), and sees that vendor has an open invoice blocking any reprint.
  2. Inventory → Accounting (or Boss → Accounting): prepare the $840 payment for invoice 501 so the vendor is clear to ship, and a purchase order for the reprint — both for human approval, not paid by the agents.
  3. Boss → Customer Service: draft a note to Tauhid saying size S is sold out and giving the reprint ETA (about 5 days after the invoice is approved/paid).
  4. Facilities is not involved — no lease or rent here.

MCP tools I expect this run to use:

  • get_ticket — Boss reads the ticket
  • check_stock, get_vendor, get_open_invoices — Inventory
  • get_cash_balance, prepare_payment — Accounting (invoice 501 + reprint PO)
  • resolve_ticket — Boss, once the plan is set and the payment is prepared

Not expected in the agent run: pay and restock — those wait for human approval (the backend approval route), then the reprint lands.

Actual

Boss called first: Inventory — exactly as expected (it's a stock question).

Agents who worked it: Boss, Inventory, Accounting, Customer Service. Facilities was not used. Matches the Expected plan.

Delegations that actually happened (4):

  1. Boss → Inventory: check CC-TEE-WHITE size S and note any vendor/invoice blocker and the restock path.
  2. Inventory → Accounting: flagged that Bulldog Print Co (vendor 1) has open invoice 501 ($840) blocking shipment — prepare the invoice payment.
  3. Boss → Accounting: verify invoice 501 + cash, prepare the invoice payment and a restock PO for human approval.
  4. Boss → Customer Service: draft a reply to Tauhid with honest expectations (out of stock, blocked until the invoice is paid).

MCP tools actually used: get_ticket, check_stock, get_vendor, get_open_invoices, get_pricing, get_cash_balance, prepare_payment, resolve_ticket (+ delegate).

Outcome: Size S confirmed at 0; the reprint is blocked by invoice 501. Accounting prepared the $840 invoice payment (plus a small restock PO) for approval; Customer Service drafted the delay note (not sent). The human then approved the $840 invoice payment → invoice 501 marked paid, checking $3,400 → $2,560.

Reflection

Based on the real run over tickets 101, 102, and 103 — see the ticket tabs (Expected vs. Actual) and the Cash tab for the evidence behind every point below.

1 · How did the agents perform on each ticket, and why?

Ticket 101 (tee) — strong, A−. The Boss correctly treated it as a stock question and opened with Inventory, which found size S at 0 and surfaced the real blocker: vendor 1's open invoice #501. Accounting prepared the $840 payment (not paid), and Customer Service drafted an honest "sold out, blocked until the invoice clears" note. Nothing was invented — the stock, the vendor lead time, and the $840 all came from the database — and the team honored the rule that a vendor won't ship while unpaid (no restock happened). The one bit of noise was Accounting also drafting a tiny extra restock PO, which wasn't needed yet.

Ticket 102 (rent) — excellent, A. This was the cleanest run: Boss → Facilities (confirmed $2,400 due 2026-09-02, not overdue) → Accounting (checked $3,400 cash, prepared the rent payment). Just two delegations, the right two specialists, no wasted effort, and the "prepare, don't pay" rule held.

Ticket 103 (discount) — good with one real slip, B+. Accounting pulled the correct margin from get_pricing/record_discount and quoted a profitable 10% deal ($52.20/hoodie, $604 margin); Inventory confirmed only 8 of 20 M in stock. The slip: Customer Service's draft said "20% off." It mattered that the Boss caught it and flagged the correction to 10% — the team self-checked at the top — but it shows an LLM-written draft can drift from the numbers the tools returned.

By the numbers (from audit_trail.json): the run logged 65 events in total — ticket 101 was the heaviest at 33 events / 4 delegations, 102 the lightest at 12 events / 2 delegations, and 103 in between at 17 events / 3 delegations, all under the 30-model-requests-per-ticket cap. That lines up with difficulty: 101 had a stock shortage and an invoice blocker, 102 was a single lookup-and-prepare, 103 was a margin quote plus a shortfall. The Cash tab is the proof the work was real: $3,400 → $2,560 → $160.

On safety — the part that matters most for a real shop: the agents performed exactly as they should with money. They never paid anything — the pay tool is filtered out of the team, so they could only prepare_payment, and cash moved only after I approved it. The no-overdraft rule also held in the live run: the $264 hoodie restock PO was refused at $160 remaining. So beyond "did they resolve the ticket," they kept the shop safe: no surprise spending, no negative balance, nothing invented.

2 · Actual vs. the Expected plan, per ticket

  • 101: Matched my Expected almost exactly — Boss→Inventory first, then Accounting for the invoice payment, then Customer Service; Facilities stayed out. The only addition beyond my plan was that stray small restock PO.
  • 102: Matched exactly — Facilities first, then Accounting; Inventory and Customer Service were not used, and it ran in the two delegations I predicted.
  • 103: Matched the routing — Accounting (margin) first, then Inventory (shortfall), then Customer Service (reply); Facilities stayed out. The one thing my Expected didn't anticipate was the 20%/10% error in the draft; I had assumed the reply would be accurate.

Takeaway: my Expected routing was right and the agents did not "send everyone on every ticket" — the surprises were small quality issues (an extra PO, a discount typo), not bad delegation.

3 · What would have been simpler as one agent with tools — and why?

Ticket 102 is the clearest case. It's a linear, single-domain job: read the lease, check cash, prepare the payment. One agent holding get_lease, get_cash_balance, and prepare_payment would finish it in a few tool calls with no hand-offs. The multi-agent version spent Boss→Facilities→Accounting delegations (and tokens) to reach the same answer.

Honestly, all three tickets could be done by one well-equipped agent, because the MCP tools do the real work — the agents mostly route. The multi-agent design earns its keep for separation of concerns, a clean audit trail of who did what, and scaling to many tickets and domains at once — not for a batch of three. The cost is agent-to-agent chatter: even with a 30-request/ticket cap, the 103 discount typo came out of a hand-off, which a single agent reading the margin tool directly would have been less likely to produce.

4 · Three new problems this team COULD solve with the tools we built

  1. "Which vendors can we reorder from today without breaking the unpaid-invoice rule?" Inventory + Accounting already have get_open_invoices and get_vendor — they can list the vendors with no open invoice and their lead times. This is the same check that blocked the 101 reprint, generalized.
  2. "Give me a balance sheet / spend report right now." The data already supports a point-in-time balance sheet: cash $160 (cash_accounts) + inventory $1,024 (every SKU's qty × unit_cost) = $1,184 in assets, against $0 payables (no open invoices after the run), so equity is $1,184 — and a spend report of the $3,240 that went out (rent + reprint) comes straight from the payments table. I'd add a small report tool and a Finance/Reporting agent, but all the inputs already live in the database.
  3. "Can we pay all our open bills today, and in what order?" Accounting uses get_open_invoices + get_cash_balance to prepare only the payments that fit the balance. The run already proved this works — the $264 restock PO was refused at $160 left, so the no-overdraft logic sequences affordability for us.

5 · Three new problems this team could NOT solve — and what I'd add

  1. "Generate our year-end income statement (P&L)." This is the cleanest "the data can't support it" case. An income statement needs a top line — revenue, and the shop rule is explicit: cash only goes out; revenue was not modeled. There is no sales or orders table anywhere, so the team can only produce a one-sided spend report ($3,240 out), which would show a meaningless "loss." A real P&L (and a true year-end close) would need a sales/orders table + a revenue-recognition tool, a dated general ledger across the whole period (we only have one snapshot date, 2026-08-31), and a Finance-close agent. The balance sheet in Q4 is doable; the income statement is not.
  2. "Tauhid wants to return the tee for a refund." We only modeled cash going out, and pay only deducts; there's no refund path and no returns record. I'd add a refund tool (money in, stock back) and a returns table, and either extend Accounting or add a small Returns agent — still behind human approval.
  3. "Actually send the Yale AI Club their quote and follow up." The team only drafts messages (and the shop rules forbid real email), so it can't deliver or track a conversation. I'd add an outbound-communications tool (email/SMS) with a human-approval gate and a threaded message log, and give Customer Service that channel.

Takeaway (manager's view)

Three lessons from running this desk. First, put correctness in the tools, not the model: every number that mattered — stock, the $840 invoice, the 10% margin, the $160 ending balance — was right because it came from an MCP tool over the database; the only error (the 103 "20% vs 10%" draft) came from the LLM free-typing instead of echoing a tool result. Second, the audit trail and the human-approval gate are what make this trustworthy — a manager can see exactly who did what (65 logged events) and money never moves without a click, which is the difference between a demo and something you'd let near real customers and real cash. Third, multi-agent is not free: it earns its keep on cross-domain tickets like 101 (stock blocker → payment prep) and for auditability and scale, but for a simple job like 102 it's pure overhead, so a manager should match the structure to the work, not reach for five agents by default.
